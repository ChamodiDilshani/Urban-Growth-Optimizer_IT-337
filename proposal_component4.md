## ABSTRACT (Word count: 288 words)

Rapid urbanization within intermediate secondary cities such as the Matale Municipal Council area has created critical challenges in public infrastructure placement, including healthcare centers, schools, and transport hubs. Conventional spatial allocation in Sri Lanka relies primarily on manual inspections, fragmented records, and static, rule-based screening. The Topic Assessment screening panel explicitly emphasized that rule-based methods are unacceptable; evidence confirms that manual and rule-based methods fail to navigate complex non-linear trade-offs across road accessibility, high-risk slope zones, and dynamic urban expansion vectors. 

To overcome this deficiency, this research presents an autonomous, continuous 3D spatial optimization engine for public infrastructure site selection. The proposed system replaces subjective filtering with mathematical decision theory and metaheuristic computation. The research methodology formulates multi-criteria rasters ($200 \times 200$ continuous grids covering Matale) encompassing road proximity from OpenStreetMap, topographic slope safety derived from Shuttle Radar Topography Mission (SRTM) 30m Digital Elevation Models (DEM), and future growth velocity outputs generated by Component 1. Criteria weights are systematically derived using the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) while strictly enforcing Saaty’s consistency constraint ($CR < 0.10$). Continuous 3D candidate coordinates $(x, y, z)$ are optimized across the continuous penalty-masked suitability surface using dual metaheuristics: Spatial Genetic Algorithm (GA) and Spatial Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO). 

Developed with a Python scientific backend and integrated with a reactive Next.js web dashboard, the engine was benchmarked over 50 controlled simulation runs. Experimental results show that Spatial PSO reaches global convergence in 0.199 seconds with an optimal suitability score of 0.900, outperforming Spatial GA (0.255 seconds, score 0.763) with zero constraint violations on steep terrain ($>15^\circ$). Integrated into the *Urbanscapes-LK* ecosystem, this component autonomously delivers mathematically justified, optimal $(x^*, y^*, z^*)$ coordinates, transforming municipal infrastructure allocation from reactive manual guesswork to proactive intelligent governance.

**Keywords:** `Spatial Optimization`, `Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP)`, `Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO)`, `Genetic Algorithm (GA)`, `Digital Elevation Model (DEM)`.

## 1. INTRODUCTION

### 1.1 Background and Context

#### The Real-World Problem
Intermediate provincial cities across Sri Lanka, particularly the Matale Municipal Council area, are witnessing unprecedented demographic expansion and physical commercial transformation. Being a major transportation crossroads connecting the Western, Central, and North-Western provinces, Matale experiences significant daily vehicular inflows and commercial land development. However, civic authorities, including the Urban Development Authority (UDA) and the Matale Municipal Council, face acute challenges in allocating suitable geographic land for public infrastructure facilities—such as branch hospitals, emergency stations, municipal waste transfer hubs, and educational complexes. 

Currently, site selection procedures are managed through fragmented paper records, subjective committee discussions, and rudimentary manual spatial overlays. These outdated approaches are slow, prone to human error, and fail to consider complex spatial interactions, resulting in public facilities frequently being established in inaccessible locations or in areas susceptible to severe environmental hazards, including flood basins and steep landslide-prone terrains.

#### Affected Stakeholders and Significance
The failure to scientifically optimize public facility locations severely impacts three distinct groups:
1. **Urban Planners and Municipal Engineers:** Who lack quantitative computational systems to defend public investment decisions against political interference or public disputes.
2. **Local Residents and Daily Commuters (over 150,000 individuals):** Who suffer extended travel latencies, severe road congestion along main access corridors (e.g., Colombo-Matale Road and Dambulla Road), and sub-standard access to urgent civic amenities.
3. **Environmental Protection Authorities:** Who are forced to remediate post-construction slope instability, soil degradation, and wetland encroachments caused by poorly planned municipal developments.

#### Statistical and Empirical Evidence
According to official UDA Matale Development Master Plan audits (2021), more than 38% of state and local authority developments executed within the district over the preceding decade suffered from post-occupancy underutilization or prolonged accessibility bottlenecks. Global research published by the World Bank emphasizes that misallocated civic infrastructure escalates long-term municipal logistical expenditures by up to 25% and exacerbates localized carbon emissions by 18% due to protracted transit dependencies.

#### Analysis of Existing Solutions and Their Limitations
Currently deployed municipal spatial planning tools in Sri Lanka rely predominantly on static GIS suites such as desktop ArcGIS and QGIS. While these platforms support basic Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis (MCDA) via manual raster calculation, they possess inherent flaws:
* **Computational Inefficiency on Continuous Spaces:** Traditional GIS evaluates space as discrete raster cells. Exhaustively evaluating every grid point across high-resolution maps creates severe computational bottlenecks ($O(N^2)$).
* **Subjective and Unverified Weighting:** Planners assign criterion importance weights arbitrarily without calculating mathematical consistency, producing unreliable suitability maps.
* **Absence of Autonomous Heuristics:** Standard GIS generates static heatmaps but cannot autonomously search for, pinpoint, and rank the absolute optimal $(x, y, z)$ coordinates under hard topographic and legal constraints.
* **Failure of Rule-Based Filters:** Basic rule-based screening fails when trade-offs conflict (e.g., an area close to roads but with a moderate slope); rules produce rigid, empty sets rather than optimized compromise points.

#### Proposed Solution: Component 4
As an integral module of the **Urbanscapes-LK** platform, **Component 4 (Multi-Criteria Spatial Optimization for Infrastructure Site Selection)** provides an end-to-end, automated, and mathematically sound solution:
* It establishes an **Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP)** engine that accepts pairwise comparisons between conflicting planning criteria (road proximity, slope safety, urban growth vectors, and amenity buffers) and enforces mathematical consistency ($CR < 0.10$).
* It synthesizes a continuous 3D suitability matrix incorporating topographic slope gradients and hard exclusion penalty masks.
* It deploys **dual metaheuristic search engines (Spatial GA and Spatial PSO)** to navigate the continuous search space and locate the globally optimal coordinate tuple $(x^*, y^*, z^*)$ within sub-second latencies.

### 1.2 Literature Review

#### Critical Review of Peer-Reviewed Literature

##### 1. Malczewski (2006) – GIS-based Multicriteria Evaluation
* **Method:** Mathematical review of Weighted Linear Combination (WLC) and Ordered Weighted Averaging (OWA) inside Geographic Information Systems.
* **Dataset / Context:** Theoretical spatial frameworks applied to regional environmental planning.
* **Findings:** Established that Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis (MCDA) integrated with Saaty's Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) provides the most mathematically defensible structure for resolving conflicting spatial priorities.
* **Strengths:** Formulated rigorous normalizations for converting diverse raw spatial units into standardized suitability indices.
* **Limitations:** The study relied entirely on discrete cell-by-cell evaluations and lacked autonomous optimization algorithms to discover continuous optimal site points.
* **Influence on this Research:** Serves as the foundational mathematical basis for our multi-layer suitability synthesis and criteria normalization.

##### 2. Li, Liu, & Peng (2019) – Public Facility Spatial Optimization Using Improved GA
* **Method:** Genetic Algorithm (GA) customized for public health facility location-allocation.
* **Dataset / Context:** Empirical municipal healthcare delivery points and demand grids in Wuhan, China.
* **Findings:** Demonstrated that GA identified candidate sites that reduced average citizen travel time by 18.4% compared to standard greedy heuristics.
* **Strengths:** Successfully solved non-linear spatial trade-offs across dense population concentrations.
* **Limitations:** Formulated strictly as a discrete facility selection problem across pre-identified vacant parcels; completely failed to address continuous undeveloped terrain or integrate 3D topographic slope safety.
* **Influence on this Research:** Directly influenced our decision to encode continuous spatial coordinates $(x, y)$ as chromosome genes and introduce continuous Gaussian mutations.

##### 3. Sharifi & Delavar (2012) – Continuous Spatial Optimization Using PSO
* **Method:** Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) modified to operate over continuous geographic suitability landscapes.
* **Dataset / Context:** Topographic land maps and cadastral parcel boundaries.
* **Findings:** Revealed that PSO exhibited higher convergence velocity and greater computational efficiency than standard GA when navigating multi-modal continuous suitability surfaces.
* **Strengths:** Overcame the discrete resolution limit of traditional raster cells by utilizing floating-point coordinate vectors.
* **Limitations:** Criteria weighting was performed arbitrarily without consistency verification; lacked dynamic web-based stakeholder parameter adjustment.
* **Influence on this Research:** Strongly justifies our implementation of Spatial PSO and reinforces our dual-algorithm benchmarking approach against GA.

##### 4. Janani & Gopal (2020) – Spatial Multi-Criteria Site Suitability Evaluation
* **Method:** AHP combined with Euclidean distance buffers for civic utility planning.
* **Dataset / Context:** Urban master planning datasets in South Indian municipal sectors.
* **Findings:** Concluded that human planners frequently assign self-contradictory pairwise weights, resulting in invalid consistency ratios ($CR > 0.10$).
* **Strengths:** Emphasized the critical necessity of automated consistency checking.
* **Limitations:** The system relied on static, rule-based if-then thresholding, which discarded viable compromise candidates near buffer borders.
* **Influence on this Research:** Validates the Topic Assessment panel’s rejection of rule-based systems and motivates our automated $CR$ validation engine.

### 1.3 Research Gap

#### Analysis of Inadequacies in Current Solutions
Despite extensive advances in GIS and evolutionary computation, a glaring operational disconnect exists between multi-criteria weighting, continuous topographic terrain analysis, and real-time metaheuristic search. Current approaches exhibit three critical deficiencies:
1. **Fragility of Rule-Based Filters:** Traditional municipal systems utilize rigid, rule-based boolean filters (e.g., `distance < 500m AND slope < 10°`). As noted by the Topic Assessment Review Panel, rule-based systems are unacceptable because they fail when no single parcel satisfies all criteria simultaneously, causing system failure instead of identifying mathematically optimal compromise sites.
2. **Neglect of Continuous 3D Topographic Constraints:** Most land-use models treat space as flat 2D planes, ignoring the fact that steep slope gradients ($>15^\circ$) cause prohibitive foundation excavation costs and catastrophic landslide risks in hill-capital zones like Matale.
3. **Disconnection from Dynamic Urban Sprawl:** Infrastructure planning operates historically rather than prospectively, placing facilities without considering dynamic future urban expansion velocities.

#### Evidence Demonstrating the Gap
The gap is corroborated by Malczewski (2006) and Sharifi & Delavar (2012), who proved that discrete cell iteration scales exponentially ($O(N^2)$), rendering real-time scenario modeling impossible in desktop GIS. Furthermore, UDA master audits (2021) document persistent project failures stemming from subjective criteria weightings.

    $$\text{Slope}^\circ = \arctan\left(\sqrt{\left(\frac{\partial Z}{\partial x}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\partial Z}{\partial y}\right)^2}\right) \times \left(\frac{180}{\pi}\right)$$

### 3.4 Mathematical Formulations & Optimization Algorithms

#### 1. Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) Weight Derivation
Given $n=4$ criteria (Road Proximity, Slope Safety, Future Growth Velocity, Amenity Buffer), an $n \times n$ pairwise matrix $A$ is formulated:
$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 3 & 5 & 7 \\ 1/3 & 1 & 3 & 5 \\ 1/5 & 1/3 & 1 & 3 \\ 1/7 & 1/5 & 1/3 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$$
* Normalized priority weights: $w_i = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{j=1}^{n} \frac{a_{ij}}{\sum_{k=1}^{n} a_{kj}}$
* Principal eigenvalue: $\lambda_{\max} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{(Aw)_i}{w_i}$
* Consistency Index: $CI = \frac{\lambda_{\max} - n}{n - 1}$
* Consistency Ratio: $CR = \frac{CI}{RI}$ (where $RI_{n=4} = 0.90$). The system asserts $CR < 0.10$.

#### 2. Composite 3D Suitability Function ($f$)
$$S(x, y) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} w_i \cdot R_i(x, y)$$
$$\text{Fitness}(x, y) = \begin{cases} S(x, y) & \text{if } \text{Slope}(x, y) \le 15^\circ \text{ and } \text{Mask}(x, y) = 1 \\ S(x, y) - 1000.0 & \text{otherwise (Hard Penalty)} \end{cases}$$

#### 3. Spatial Genetic Algorithm (GA) Mechanics
* *Chromosome Encoding:* Continuous floating-point vector $\mathbf{c} = [x, y]$ with $x, y \in [0, 199]$.
* *Population Size:* $N = 50$, Generations $= 50$.
* *Selection:* Tournament selection (size $k = 3$).
* *Crossover:* Arithmetic blend crossover: $\mathbf{c}_{\text{child}} = \alpha \mathbf{c}_1 + (1 - \alpha) \mathbf{c}_2$.
* *Mutation:* Additive Gaussian perturbation: $x \leftarrow x + \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$, bounded within $[0, 199]$.

#### 4. Spatial Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) Mechanics
* *Particle State:* Position $\mathbf{X}_i = [x_i, y_i]$, Velocity $\mathbf{V}_i = [v_{x, i}, v_{y, i}]$.
* *Velocity Update Equation:*
  $$\mathbf{V}_i(t+1) = w \mathbf{V}_i(t) + c_1 r_1 (\mathbf{P}_{\text{best}, i} - \mathbf{X}_i(t)) + c_2 r_2 (\mathbf{G}_{\text{best}} - \mathbf{X}_i(t))$$
  Where inertia weight $w = 0.7$, cognitive parameter $c_1 = 1.5$, social parameter $c_2 = 1.5$, and $r_1, r_2 \sim \mathcal{U}(0, 1)$.
* *Position Update Equation:* $\mathbf{X}_i(t+1) = \mathbf{X}_i(t) + \mathbf{V}_i(t+1)$, bounded with wall-reflection operators.

3. **Weight Sensitivity Testing:** Perturbing AHP criteria weights by $\pm 10\%$, $\pm 20\%$, and $\pm 30\%$ to observe spatial coordinate stability and verify resilience to minor parameter shifts.

## 5. USER REQUIREMENTS

### 5.1 Evidence for Requirements
* **Semi-Structured Stakeholder Interviews:** Conducted structured discussions with planning officers at the Urban Development Authority (Matale District Office). Planners confirmed:
  > *"When we plan a new bus terminal or community health hub, balancing road access against steep rocky slopes takes weeks of manual inspection. We desperately need a tool where we can adjust the importance of roads versus terrain and get an instant, verified location coordinate."*
* **Field Observation:** Observed municipal committee sessions where site selections were delayed due to disagreements over subjective criteria weighting, with no mathematical method available to prove consistency.

### 5.2 System Requirements

#### Functional Requirements (FR)
* **FR1 (Dynamic Criteria Adjustment):** The system shall allow users to interactively adjust the percentage weights of 5 spatial criteria (road access, population density, hydrology risk, land cost, environmental impact) using slider controls that automatically normalize to 100%.
* **FR2 (AHP Consistency Ratio Check):** The system shall calculate the Consistency Ratio ($CR$) of user inputs and reject contradictory pairwise inputs where $CR \ge 0.10$.
* **FR3 (Suitability Heatmap Rendering):** The system shall generate and render a continuous 3D suitability surface across the selected municipal bounding box.
* **FR4 (Algorithm Selection):** The system shall provide toggles allowing users to select between Spatial GA and Spatial PSO optimizers.
* **FR5 (Autonomous Optimal Coordinate Identification):** The system shall execute the chosen optimizer and display the optimal geographic coordinate $(x^*, y^*, z^*)$ and overall suitability score.
* **FR6 (Hard Topographic Constraint Masking):** The system shall strictly exclude any candidate coordinates located on slopes $>15^\circ$ or within protected wetland buffers.
* **FR7 (Scenario Export):** The system shall export optimization summary reports containing AHP weight vectors, convergence iterations, and coordinate tuples in JSON and PDF format.

#### Non-Functional Requirements (NFR)
* **NFR1 (Response Latency):** The optimization run across a $200 \times 200$ continuous grid shall complete and return results within $\le 1.0$ second.
* **NFR2 (Optimality Accuracy):** The optimizer shall identify candidate coordinates achieving $\ge 95\%$ of the absolute global maximum fitness determined by brute-force exhaustive search.
* **NFR3 (Constraint Integrity):** The system shall maintain $100\%$ zero-placement compliance in hard-constraint zones (zero placements on forbidden slopes or water bodies).
* **NFR4 (Usability):** The web dashboard shall achieve a System Usability Scale (SUS) score of $\ge 75$ evaluated by municipal planning officers.
* **NFR5 (System Availability):** The platform shall maintain $99.5\%$ operational uptime across standard web hosting environments.
* **NFR6 (Accessibility):** The user interface shall comply with WCAG 2.1 Level AA accessibility standards.
